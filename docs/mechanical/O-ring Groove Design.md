1) Always go with O-rings of this type: Water- and steam-resistant Parker E0603 EPDM O-Ring

2) McMaster-Carr is your best bet for vendors. Design your grooves based off O-ring profiles that exist. Estimate the groove geometry that is necessary by having having an estimate of the     geometry you intend on enclosing.

3) This formula is useful for ensuring the groove with actually have the O-ring fit inside 2l_1+2l_2 + 4r = inner or outer circumference of O-ring. This can either be for the inner groove or the outer groove. It depends on whether the sub uses a vacuum or positive pressure in the chamber. Reference Parker O-ring handbook. The image above is pretty useful and has the select information necessary. Flo uses an internal pressure of 5 PSI that will experience a pressure drop of 3.03 psi at 7 feet depth. Any seals for Flo should therefore use the outside diameter of the O-ring.

4) Groove width, W, depends on the squeeze and cross-sectional diameter of the O-ring (often denoted as the width of the O-ring). Aim for 20 percent squeeze.

5) For race track grooves. The value of r should be within a range of 3-6 x times the cross-sectional diameter of the O-ring.

6) O-rings only work if there is enough force to compress the O-ring. Each O-ring thickness has a force per linear length minimum requirement that must be met in order for the seal to hold. Multiply the circumference of the O-ring to the force per linear length to get how much force you need. A case study of this is the O-ring for the pressure hull of Flo. The latches actually provide enough clamping force to meet that requirement with a safety factor.
